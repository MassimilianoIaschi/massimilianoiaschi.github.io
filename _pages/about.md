<p>
  My first real passions were <strong>poetry</strong> and <strong>art</strong>. During my first year of high school I used to spend hours inside of art museums everyday during summer, sometimes even just for looking at a couple of paintings.
  Studying <strong> art</strong> allowed me to discover <strong>Leonardo da Vinci</strong>. Studying<strong> Leonardo</strong> allowed me to discover <strong>robotics</strong>. Studying <strong> robotics</strong> allowed me to discover <strong>my future</strong>.
</p>


<p>
  While <strong>currently at Prof. Marco Hutter's ETH RSL lab</strong> until January, I'm a senior undergraduate student at <strong>Georgia Institute of Technology</strong>, graduating in <strong>Mechanical Engineering</strong> in Spring 2026 while minoring in both <strong>robotics</strong> and <strong>biology/bio-inspired design</strong>, as well as an undergraduate researcher at <strong>Prof. Daniel I. Goldman's CRAB Lab</strong>, active from Fall 2023 to the beginning of Fall 2025, summers included. Rigth before joining the CRAB Lab, in summer 2023 I was a research intern at <strong>Roland Siegwart's Autonomous Systems Lab</strong> at <strong>ETH Zurich</strong>, working on the autonomous underwater robotics project Tethys. In summer 2022 I was a research intern at <strong>Domenico Prattichizzo's SIRSLab</strong> at <strong>Siena University</strong>, working on <strong>robotic haptic applications</strong>.
</p>

<p>
  Within my undergraduate research my focus has been on <strong>bio-inspired robotics terrestrial locomotion</strong>, allowing me to gain experience in <strong>mechatronics and bio-inspired design, control systems design, physics-based modeling, and more recently robotics simulation and learning</strong>.
</p>

<p>
  While in the CRAB Lab we emphasize on the mechanical intelligence of the robots rather than on their computational intelligence, and we use our robots as a tool to model experimentally the principles of their physics of locomotion, <strong>my idea is to pursue my PhD in a lab that still works at the intersection of robotics, physics, and biology, but prioritizes building systems through rigorous engineering</strong> with the objective of not only <strong>advancing science through robotics</strong>, but <strong>also of discovering solutions that could transform the state-of-the-art in exploratory/search-and-rescue/space robotics</strong>, where emphasis is given mainly to design, controls, and modeling, but also to the robot's autonomy and planning.
</p>

<p>
  Terrestrial locomotion is what I mainly worked on so far, but aerial and aquatic locomotion interest me just as much. Specifically, I share the vision of my supervisor here at ETH, Postdoc Robert Baines, of <strong>designing hybrid shape-morphing robots capable of adapting their morphology in real-time</strong> to successfully carry out their tasks across any terrain.
</p>
